What Is an Electric Low Speed Vehicle?

An electric low speed vehicle (LSV) is a federally recognized class of four-wheeled electric vehicle with a top speed of 20–25 mph and a gross vehicle weight rating under 3,000 pounds. Unlike a standard golf cart, an electric LSV is built to meet federal safety standards, which means it can be registered, titled, and driven legally on most public roads posted at 35 mph or less. Every street-legal LSV includes headlights, brake lights, turn signals, mirrors, a windshield, seat belts, and a Vehicle Identification Number (VIN).

Because they run on electric drivetrains, LSVs produce zero tailpipe emissions and cost a fraction of what a gas vehicle costs to operate. That combination of low running costs, easy maneuverability, and street-legal capability is why electric low speed vehicles have become popular in neighborhoods, retirement communities, resorts, college campuses, and downtown districts across the country.

Electric LSV vs. Golf Cart: What's the Difference?

The terms are often used interchangeably, but there is an important distinction. A traditional golf cart is not street legal and is limited to private property or golf courses. An electric LSV meets federal safety standards and can be registered for road use. Many of the street-legal golf carts our dealers sell are, in fact, LSVs — golf-cart platforms enhanced with the lighting, restraints, and equipment required to make them legal on public streets. If you want something you can actually drive to the store or around the neighborhood, an electric low speed vehicle is what you are looking for.

Electric LSV Frequently Asked Questions

What is an electric low speed vehicle (LSV)?

An electric low speed vehicle (LSV) is a federally classified four-wheeled electric vehicle with a top speed of 20–25 mph and a gross vehicle weight rating under 3,000 lbs. LSVs are street legal on most roads posted at 35 mph or less.

Are electric LSVs street legal?

Yes. Electric LSVs are street legal on roads posted at 35 mph or less when equipped with required safety features such as headlights, turn signals, mirrors, a windshield, and seat belts. Local registration rules vary by state.

What is the difference between an electric LSV and a golf cart?

A standard golf cart is not street legal, while an electric LSV meets federal safety standards and can be registered for road use. Many street-legal golf carts sold by our dealers are upgraded to LSV specifications.
