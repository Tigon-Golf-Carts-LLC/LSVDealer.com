Street Legal Electric Vehicle: LSVs That Meet DOT Road Requirements

How low speed vehicles qualify as road-legal electric vehicles

A street legal electric vehicle lets you drive on public roads without a full-size car — and the most accessible street legal electric vehicle for most buyers is the low speed vehicle (LSV). This guide explains what makes an electric vehicle street legal, how LSVs qualify, which roads they may use, and how road rules vary by state, so you can buy a street legal electric vehicle with confidence.

What Makes an Electric Vehicle Street Legal?

To be a street legal electric vehicle, a vehicle must meet the federal safety standard for its class and be eligible for registration. Low speed vehicles are governed by Federal Motor Vehicle Safety Standard (FMVSS) No. 500, which sets the equipment a street legal electric LSV must have: headlights, turn signals, tail and brake lamps, reflectors, mirrors, a windshield, a parking brake, seat belts, and a 17-character VIN. Beyond the vehicle itself, being street legal also means meeting your state's requirements — registration, insurance, and a valid driver's license.

How LSVs Qualify as Street Legal Electric Vehicles

An LSV is purpose-built to be a street legal electric vehicle. Because it leaves the factory equipped to FMVSS No. 500 and carries a VIN, it can be titled and registered like any road vehicle. That is the key difference between a street legal electric LSV and an ordinary golf cart, which is not road-legal. When a dealer advertises a "street-legal golf cart," they are describing a low speed vehicle that qualifies as a street legal electric vehicle.

Which Roads Can a Street Legal LSV Use?

In most states, a street legal electric vehicle in the LSV class may be driven on roads with a posted speed limit of 35 mph or less. Some states permit LSVs to cross higher-speed roads at intersections, while some localities set additional limits. Because these rules are set at the state and local level, always confirm where you may drive before you buy.

Street Legal Electric Vehicle FAQ

What makes an electric vehicle street legal?

A street legal electric vehicle must meet the federal safety standard for its class and be registrable for road use. For low speed vehicles, that standard is FMVSS No. 500, which requires headlights, turn signals, mirrors, seat belts, a windshield, a parking brake, and a VIN. With that equipment, plus state registration, insurance, and a driver's license, an electric LSV is a street legal electric vehicle.

Are LSVs street legal electric vehicles?

Yes. A low speed vehicle built to FMVSS No. 500 is a street legal electric vehicle on roads posted at 35 mph or less in most states. It can be titled, registered, and insured like other road vehicles. Exact rules — including which roads you may use — vary by state and locality.

What roads can a street legal electric vehicle use?

In most states, a street legal LSV may operate on roads with a posted speed limit of 35 mph or less, and in some states may cross higher-speed roads at intersections. Local ordinances can add or narrow where LSVs are permitted, so confirm the rules with your state DMV and municipality.
